Both models agreed that our topic was relevant, the video was organized, and we explained both the benefits and risks of AI coding assistants. They also agreed that participation from every member was not completely clear.

The main difference was the grading. Gemini gave us **85/100**, while ChatGPT gave us **40/100**. Gemini gave high marks for the AI evaluation and reflection even though those sections were not completed yet. ChatGPT was stricter and only gave credit for what it could verify. I found ChatGPT’s feedback more useful because it clearly explained what was missing.

## A4. Do You Agree With the AI’s Grade?

I do not completely agree with either grade. Gemini’s score was too high because it gave us credit for work that had not been completed. ChatGPT’s score felt too low when considering the quality of our research and video, but it was more accurate based on what was available at the time.

The grading process was confusing because we needed the AI evaluations before we could complete the comparison and reflection. The 45-point difference also surprised me and showed how differently AI models can judge the same project.

## A5. What Would You Do Differently?

If we redid the project, we would organize the repository earlier, make sure every member had meaningful commits and a speaking part, and save our AI prompts and responses from the beginning. We would also add better references and direct links to all project files in the README.

I do not think the video needs to be much longer because it was meant to be a short presentation. We would focus more on equal participation instead of adding unnecessary information.

# Part B — AI Research Assistance Reflection

## B1. How We Used AI During Research

We used **Gemini and ChatGPT** to brainstorm ideas, organize our research, simplify technical explanations, and improve the wording in our presentation. We also used both models to evaluate the final project using the same rubric.

## B2. What Worked Well

AI was most useful for organizing our topic into clear sections, including the history, tools, benefits, risks, and future impact of coding assistants.

For example, we asked AI to explain how coding tools developed from basic autocomplete into modern agentic systems. The timeline it provided helped us structure that part of the video.

## B3. What Did Not Work Well

AI sometimes made broad claims about productivity and code quality without providing strong evidence. We had to review and rewrite those statements more carefully.

Gemini’s grading was another example. It gave high scores for the AI evaluation and reflection even though those parts were missing. This showed us that AI may make assumptions when it does not have enough information.

## B4. Overall Reflection

AI helped us work faster by supporting our brainstorming, organization, and writing. However, we still had to check the information and decide whether its suggestions were accurate and useful.

For a similar project, we would still use AI, but we would save our prompts from the beginning and verify important claims using reliable sources. The biggest lesson was that AI can sound confident while still being incorrect or making assumptions, so human judgment is always necessary.
